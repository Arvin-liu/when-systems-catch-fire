## Exact question

In adults self-studying a fixed set of unfamiliar factual items, with total learning time and retrieval count held equal, does spaced retrieval versus one massed session increase mean proportion correct on a seven-day delayed test?

## Scope and estimand

- Population: adults learning non-high-stakes unfamiliar factual items in public randomized, quasi-randomized or well-described controlled studies.
- Eligible delay: seven days; near-seven-day outcomes are context/measurement-mismatch rows unless directly comparable.
- Outcome: study-reported average treatment effect or comparable between-condition difference in delayed item-level proportion correct.
- No de novo pooled meta-analysis unless data and estimands are directly comparable and the method is separately recorded.
- Ceiling: source-scoped conditional evidence for the stated adult/factual-item/schedule/delayed-test scope only; no universal education, long-term achievement or unqualified causal claim.

## Measurement and source rules

Preserve immediate recall vs delayed retention, item accuracy vs confidence, total learning time vs retrieval count, seven-day vs other delays, factual items vs skills, and randomized vs observational evidence as separate dimensions.

Minimum target: three independent source families, including at least two primary experimental/controlled-study families where available. A source family is an independent research team/data collection/original cohort and protocol; duplicate papers or repositories from one dataset do not count twice.

Include adults, unfamiliar factual items, spaced retrieval and massed comparison, delayed testing, public/auditable access, and enough design detail to classify schedule/time equality. Exclude children-only samples, high-stakes interventions, private data, immediate-only outcomes, non-factual skills and inaccessible claims with no auditable metadata.

## Contradiction and negative-evidence plan

Search for null, no-difference, failed-replication, massed-better, moderator, boundary and selective-reporting evidence. Actively test total-time confounding, retrieval-count confounding, item difficulty, prior knowledge, delay length, test format, confidence/accuracy mismatch and classroom/platform differences.

Typed outcomes include `BLOCKED_BY_EVIDENCE_ACCESS`, `NOT_IDENTIFIABLE_WITH_AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE`, `ABSTAIN`, and explicit residuals. The process comparison may conclude `NO_INCREMENTAL_VALUE_OBSERVED`.
