# 002 analysis — outcomes are conditional, not a device universal

## Analytic question

R1 placed EEG/process findings, immediate quiz results, later achievement, review, and note quantity into one “handwriting versus keyboard” question. The R2 analysis treats them as different estimands. The central test is whether the sources can support one device ranking after these estimands are separated. They cannot.

## Evidence-to-outcome table

## Reconciliation of apparently conflicting results

The direct replication and the broad meta-analysis are not measuring exactly the same thing. S3 was designed to detect a large immediate encoding advantage in a standardized lecture-and-quiz context. Its estimate was close to zero and its uncertainty included small advantages and disadvantages. S4 aggregates more varied designs, includes delayed outcomes, and codes review opportunity. Its small positive pooled estimate can therefore coexist with S3: the average across heterogeneous study conditions may be nonzero even when a well-controlled immediate replication does not show a practically large contrast.

The original S2 result also does not make the contradiction disappear. Study 1 found a conceptual longhand advantage after a short delay while factual performance was equal. Study 3 found a review interaction rather than a simple device main effect. The most coherent reading is that encoding strategy, test type, review, lecture structure, and transcription capacity jointly determine the observed contrast. “Handwriting” is a bundle of behaviors in many studies, not a single isolated causal component.

S1 adds a second boundary. Different neural signatures are compatible with similar immediate test performance. This is not a failed result: it shows that process sensitivity and behavioral sensitivity have different thresholds. But it blocks the common inferential jump from “more or different EEG activity” to “better learning.” There is no delayed outcome in S1 to supply that bridge.

## Quantitative synthesis and heterogeneity

S4 reports Hedges g=.248 overall, g=.229 for immediate achievement, and g=.276 for delayed achievement. S6 reports g=.144 with a 95% CI [.023,.265]. The estimates point in the same broad direction but differ in size. Prior syntheses cited by S4 range from approximately zero to .250. This dispersion is itself evidence against presenting a single settled effect size without specifying the evidence base.

The effect-size difference is small relative to the note-volume difference (S4 g=.919). Thus the clearest device contrast is behavioral production: keyboards make it easier to capture more words and more verbatim material. Whether that production is beneficial depends on what happens after capture. If a learner reviews and reorganizes an external record, quantity can be an asset; if immediate conceptual encoding is the only opportunity and verbatim capture displaces processing, it can be a liability. The same observed note volume can therefore sit on either side of a later-learning pathway.

## Causal interpretation

The strongest causal statement available is narrow: in controlled lecture-and-test tasks, the device and its associated note-taking behavior can change note content and process measures, while the achievement effect is small, heterogeneous, and sensitive to assessment and review conditions. A causal claim that “handwriting improves learning” requires a specified outcome and time horizon; a causal claim that “typing harms learning” fails for the external-storage and accessibility cases and is not supported by the direct replication.

The review moderator cannot be read as a randomized intervention because review opportunity is a study-level condition correlated with lecture, test, instruction, and participant characteristics. Similarly, typing skill is a plausible effect modifier but S5 is not sufficiently reported to estimate it. Familiarity covariates in S1 address one narrow process comparison; they do not establish equal device competence across populations.

## R2 provisional conclusion

`CONTEXT_DEPENDENT_COMPETING_ESTIMANDS` is the appropriate analysis state. The data support a small, conditional handwriting-associated achievement contrast in some college lecture studies, no large general immediate encoding advantage in the direct replication, clear increased typed-note quantity/verbatim capture, and a credible review-stage moderator. They do not support a universal handwriting-over-keyboard rule, a neural-to-learning inference, or a general population/accessibility recommendation.
