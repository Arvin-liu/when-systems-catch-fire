# 002 source plan

## Research identity

`MULTI_OUTCOME_EVIDENCE_SYNTHESIS`

The question is not whether one input device universally wins. The review separates six outcome families: (1) neural/process measures, (2) immediate factual and conceptual performance, (3) delayed performance, (4) review/revision and external storage, (5) note content and strategy, and (6) typing skill, familiarity, and accessibility. A result in one family cannot be silently promoted into another family.

S1 is the current-process anchor, S2/S3 are the primary experimental replication pair, and S4/S6 are quantitative syntheses with partly overlapping study populations. S5 is deliberately not treated as a complete independent estimate because its public two-page report does not expose sample size, effect size, timing, or adjustment details.

## Search and stopping rule

The source set was selected to cover the competing mechanisms and the outcomes that R1 conflated. The evidence checkpoint stops only after a full recent experiment, the original experiment, a direct replication, two independent syntheses, and a skill moderator source have been read or their limits recorded. No search-result snippet is used as evidence. A news item is not used to establish a scientific claim.

## Pre-specified ceilings

The evidence can support outcome-specific statements about process, immediate achievement, delayed achievement, review, note quantity/content, and possible skill moderation. It cannot support a universal causal ranking, a neural-to-learning causal bridge, an all-student recommendation, or a claim that externalizing notes is inherently harmful. Accessibility, disability, language, lecture design, and task familiarity remain population-boundary issues.
