# 002 limitations and open questions

1. The available experiments are short lecture-and-quiz studies, not semester-long course interventions.
2. Immediate, delayed, and review outcomes are not interchangeable; the evidence base has few authentic revision-stage measures.
3. S4 and S6 syntheses overlap in their primary literature. Their agreement is not independent replication.
4. The review moderator is study-level and confounded with instruction, assessment, and lecture design.
5. S1's EEG data are reported in the article but not publicly available for this audit; no raw-signal reanalysis was claimed.
6. S5's typing-ability report exposes only an abstract-level result. The possible interaction needs a fully reported, adequately powered study.
7. No reviewed synthesis provides a disability/accessibility analysis sufficient for a population recommendation.
8. Learner language, age, prior device skill, handwriting fluency, lecture pace, images, and external-storage behavior remain material moderators.

The next decisive design would randomize device and review opportunity, measure typing and handwriting speed, retain all notes for external review, use factual and conceptual tests at immediate and delayed points, and preregister accessibility and learner-skill interactions.
