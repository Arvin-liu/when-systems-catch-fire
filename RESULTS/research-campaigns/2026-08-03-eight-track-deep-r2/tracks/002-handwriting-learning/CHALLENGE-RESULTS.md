# 002 challenge results

This checkpoint was conducted as an adversarial review of the provisional analysis. The reviewer tried to obtain a single device winner, to turn the EEG result into a learning claim, and to treat the syntheses as independent replications. None of those moves survived the source audit.

## Challenge 1 — Does the direct replication overturn the meta-analytic signal?

No, but it narrows it. S3 is a preregistered direct replication with N=142 and equivalence bounds designed to exclude a large effect. Its factual and conceptual estimates are close to zero with intervals that include small effects. S4 aggregates 24 studies and reports g=.248 overall, while S6 reports g=.144. These estimates are not mathematically incompatible: a small average can coexist with a near-zero estimate in one standardized immediate task. The correct challenge result is therefore `NOT_OVERTURNED_BUT_SCOPE_NARROWED`, not “all evidence agrees.”

The competing explanations remain live: different tests, review opportunity, delayed timing, transcription speed, note style, lecture structure, and study overlap. The R2 report must not pool S3 with S4 as if the underlying estimates were independent.

## Challenge 2 — Is the pooled handwriting advantage just an artifact of review or timing?

Review is a plausible contributor but not a resolved cause. S4 reports a larger required-review subgroup (g=.421) than no-review (g=.208), and its moderator coefficient is .230, p=.014. However, review is a study-level feature bundled with assessment design and instruction. S2 Study 3 independently shows that review changes the pattern, but its interaction does not establish that the same effect will appear in ordinary coursework. Timing was not a significant S4 moderator, but “immediate versus delayed” is a coarse classification and cannot replace a longitudinal revision study. Finding: `MODERATOR_CANDIDATE_NOT_CAUSAL_SETTLEMENT`.

## Challenge 3 — Can EEG provide the missing causal bridge?

No. S1 reports device- and strategy-dependent EEG differences but no immediate performance difference. Its short pseudoword lectures, small Korean undergraduate sample, and absence of delayed testing leave the neural-to-learning path unmeasured. A process difference may be real and educationally relevant without implying a rank order in later achievement. Finding: `NEURAL_TO_LEARNING_INFERENCE_REJECTED`.

## Challenge 4 — Are note quantity and verbatim capture evidence of harm?

No. S2, S3, and S4 consistently show more keyboard words and more verbatim content. That is a mechanism-relevant contrast, not an outcome. External notes can support later review, accessibility, search, collaboration, or a learner who cannot write comfortably. Conversely, extra volume may reflect transcription that displaces generative processing in a no-review immediate quiz. Finding: `QUANTITY_IS_A_MEDIATOR_OR_RESOURCE_NOT_A_VERDICT`.

## Challenge 5 — Are the sources independent enough to support several confirmations?

No. S2 and S3 are directly related lines of work and S3's mini-meta includes related primary studies. S4 and S6 are syntheses over overlapping literatures. Counting all six as six independent confirmations would inflate certainty. The evidence is better represented as three evidentiary layers: primary experiments (S1–S3), pooled syntheses (S4 and S6), and a weakly exposed skill signal (S5). Finding: `SOURCE_DEPENDENCE_REQUIRES_DOWNWEIGHTING`.

## Challenge 6 — Could typing ability reverse the direction?

Possibly, but not quantified here. S5 reports a typing-ability correlation in the typing condition, yet omits the information needed to reproduce the estimate. S1 reports device familiarity differences and says the EEG result survived a familiarity covariate, but that does not measure learning moderation. A reversal or attenuation by skill is a prespecified open question, not a result to fill by intuition. Finding: `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE_FOR_SKILL_MODERATION`.

## Challenge 7 — Does the result externalize to children, Chinese learners, disability, or authentic courses?

Not from this source set. S1 uses Korean undergraduates and artificial pseudoword lectures; S2/S3 use college lecture tasks; S4 explicitly notes gaps in learner characteristics and disability needs. The population and task ceiling must remain in the final report. Finding: `EXTERNALIZATION_NOT_SUPPORTED`.

## Challenge disposition

The analysis survives only in bounded form:

- `SUPPORTED_WITH_SCOPE`: keyboards increase note quantity/verbatim capture; process signatures can differ; a small pooled achievement contrast and a review-stage moderator are plausible in the included literature.
- `CONTESTED`: the size and practical importance of any handwriting achievement advantage, especially outside controlled lecture studies.
- `NOT_SUPPORTED`: a universal handwriting-over-keyboard rule, a neural-to-learning causal leap, or the proposition that external note storage is intrinsically harmful.
- `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`: typing-skill interaction, disability/accessibility outcomes, authentic long-term course performance, and delayed neural-behavioral mediation.
