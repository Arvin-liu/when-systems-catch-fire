# Evidence brief: methane growth and emissions

NOAA reports global atmospheric CH4 annual increases of 8.29 ± 0.82 ppb in 2023, 8.04 ± 0.43 ppb in 2024, and 4.77 ± 0.43 ppb in 2025. NOAA notes that the latest year may change after recalibration and quality control. [S1]

WMO reports a 2025 globally averaged surface concentration of 1,948 ± 2 ppb and states that the 2024–2025 increase was below the 2023–2024 increase. [S2]

A 2026 GONGGA inversion paper reports a strengthened atmospheric sink relative to 2019 in 2023 and 2024 and a strong 2024 wetland-emission rebound. Its gridded posterior data are published for 2019–2024. [S3] [S4]

Independent calculation: using each monthly posterior flux, grid-cell area, and the Gregorian number of seconds in that month, the five surface-source categories sum to 578.087805 Tg CH4/year (2019), 580.397362 (2023), and 597.567348 (2024). The 2024–2023 change is +17.169986 Tg/year; the wetland component changes by +8.223196 Tg/year. These are calculations on one inversion's posterior; they are not a second inversion or a multi-model estimate. The dataset ends in 2024 and says nothing directly about 2025 emissions. [S3] [S4]

## Assessment

The observation that atmospheric methane accumulated more slowly in a later year does not by itself establish that emissions fell. Atmospheric growth reflects the balance between sources and sinks; the cited inversion specifically reports a stronger sink alongside a 2024 source rebound. This is a bounded interpretation of NOAA observations and one published inversion, not a global scientific consensus claim.
