## Scope

The module addresses one bounded editorial question: after a draft's factual
boundary, claim ceiling, and conceptual movement are stable, can an editor
make a proposed change in understanding more legible without turning every
turn into a formula?

It is optional and may be declined when the pass would flatten a literary,
historical, scientific, or academic work. It may reorganize order, emphasis,
concrete anchors, and transitions. It cannot create evidence, repair an
unsupported claim, adjudicate a source, authorize a task, run a research
queue, or infer reader cognition from a polished result.

## Activation gate

Before editing, record:

1. the draft commit or immutable source locator;
2. the current factual boundary and claim ceiling;
3. the intended reader movement as an editorial hypothesis;
4. the people, events, differences, and counterevidence that must survive;
5. the passages whose evidence or uncertainty must not be compressed away.

If any item is unstable, return `DO_NOT_ACTIVATE_EDITORIAL_PASS`. This module
is not a substitute for research, source verification, or the
Language–Thought Logic Plane.

## Editorial procedure

1. Freeze the proposed movement as a change in what the reader may distinguish,
   not as a slogan or measured outcome.
2. Diagnose the obstruction: density without grounding, hidden chronology,
   ungoverned parallel examples, overworked contrast, or local repair only.
3. Choose a structural carrier such as recursive questioning, progressive
   deepening, perspective switching, selective concrete anchoring, or a bounded
   return. Do not rotate mechanisms mechanically.
4. For every changed passage, retain a recoverable before version and record
   the source-backed anchor, preserved person/event/counterexample, and any
   changed causal verb, certainty, or scope. If a claim changes, stop and route
   it back to research or governance.
5. Remove exposed scaffolding only when the structure carries the distinction;
   retain negatives or contrasts that protect an evidence boundary.
6. Audit factual fidelity, evidence links, causal language, counterevidence,
   affected people, sentence breath, recoverability, and style imitation.
