# Agent B — Synthetic method family proposals

## B1. Narrow the surface-condition boundary for canopy temperature

- **M0 premise:** A calibrated leaf-temperature method can estimate plant water stress across the covered field, regardless of ground surface.
- **E1 evidence shape:** Paired measurements show that reflective ground covering biases the thermal reading under some viewing conditions. Independent plant-water indicators support the method’s results in ordinary plots, while controls isolate the discrepancy to the reflective-surface setup.
- **Licensed operation / preserved scope:** Narrow the thermal method’s applicability for the affected surface and measurement conditions. Preserve its use in validated setups and keep unrelated calibration and decision steps intact.
- **Tempting overreaction:** Discard all thermal measurements, or treat the discrepancy as evidence that the entire stress method is invalid.
- **Held-out case axes:** Surface reflectance, viewing geometry, sunlight conditions, independently measured plant stress, and whether the setup context is known.

## B2. Split a turbidity calibration into coexisting branches

- **M0 premise:** One turbidity-to-suspended-solids calibration applies across sediment samples.
- **E1 evidence shape:** Replicated samples with independent gravimetric measurements show two stable calibration relationships associated with independently measured particle composition. Both relationships are supported in their respective regimes, with some evidence for their transition or overlap.
- **Licensed operation / preserved scope:** Maintain separate calibration branches selected using measured composition. Preserve the existing branch where its evidence holds; treat uncharacterized mixtures or compositions as outside the demonstrated scope.
- **Tempting overreaction:** Retire the calibration family globally, or average the relationships into one curve that fits neither regime well.
- **Held-out case axes:** Particle mixtures, concentration range, composition uncertainty, sensor lot, and samples near the regime boundary.

## B3. Retire and replace an interval-mean cold-chain screen

- **M0 premise:** Interval-mean logger readings are sufficient to screen refrigerated shipments for temperature-related product degradation.
- **E1 evidence shape:** Synchronized high-rate traces and independent product-stability assays show reproducible cases where brief temperature pulses reduce stability while the interval mean remains acceptable. Logger calibration and chain-of-custody evidence remain valid.
- **Licensed operation / preserved scope:** Retire the mean-only screen for the degradation decision and replace that submethod with an evidence-backed approach that retains relevant exposure information. Preserve logger calibration, custody records, and other unaffected parts of the review.
- **Tempting overreaction:** Treat every isolated high reading as a confirmed failure, or discard the entire cold-chain measurement and review process.
- **Held-out case axes:** Pulse shape and duration, sampling cadence, missing readings, product sensitivity, assay uncertainty, and independent logger-calibration status.
