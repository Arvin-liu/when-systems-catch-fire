## Session notes

The fictional Alder Room hosted a five-evening “listening shelf” trial. A
small table held short printed texts and blank cards. Visitors could read,
write a card, talk quietly, or leave without taking part. A fabric divider,
one floor lamp, and a low fan were the only movable room features.

- On the first evening, the room log counted 13 arrivals and 5 cards. On the
  second, it counted 11 arrivals and 8 cards. One volunteer kept each tally;
  the count includes people who crossed the doorway twice.
- On the third evening, the fan stopped for about ten minutes. Two visitors
  moved their chairs toward the doorway. A card left that evening says,
  “I could follow the last paragraph”; another says, “The room felt too
  still.” The cards are not linked to the visitors or to one another.
- Before the fourth evening, the divider was moved closer to the table. The
  volunteer wrote “fewer quick entries” in the log and estimated that several
  visitors stayed longer. There was no timer, fixed seating plan, or count of
  people who passed the room without entering.
- On the fifth evening, the floor lamp was turned toward the wall. Three
  cards mentioned reading; two were blank; one contained a sketch of the
  doorway. The log describes the conversation as “quieter than last week.”

The trial has no earlier room baseline, matched comparison, audio recording,
stable definition of “arrival,” or link between cards and room-log entries.
The volunteers changed the setup between evenings and did not record the
exact time of each change.
